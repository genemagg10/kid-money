# Maggio Kids Money Dashboard — Curriculum + Screen Map
Ages now: Luca 10, Ella 8. Parent goal: funded accounts awareness → receive at 21.

## Design principles (all ages)
- Each child sees **only their** money. No sibling scoreboard.
- Parent is source of truth (Monarch / accounts). Kids view is read-only for Phase 1.
- Plain words first; real account names unlock later.
- Calm numbers. No red panic, no gamified “streaks” that shame spending.
- iPad portrait, big tap targets, outdoor-readable type.

## Three jars (map to real accounts later)
| Kid label | Meaning | Typical Maggio accounts (parent only) |
|-----------|---------|----------------------------------------|
| **Spend** | Money I can use soon | Wells kids savings (portion) — parent decides what’s “spendable” |
| **Save** | Waiting for something I want | Same savings or a goal bucket |
| **Grow** | Working for me for a long time | Brokerage + Roth + 529 (college called out inside Grow) |

Phase 1 shows Grow as one jar with optional subtitle: “Some of this is for college.”

## Phase 1 — Ages ~8–10 (ship first)
**Curriculum goals**
1. Money has jobs: use soon, wait, grow long.
2. Numbers on a screen match “my money” in the real world (trust).
3. Waiting can be a choice, not a punishment.
4. College is a long save that parents help with — not the kid’s job to fund alone.
5. Family talks: “What is this jar for?” once a week or when something changes.

**Lessons (short, one at a time — not a course dump)**
- L1: Three jars — sort pretend dollars into Spend / Save / Grow.
- L2: Your dashboard — find your name, find each jar’s number.
- L3: “This month” — something went in (gift, chore money) or stayed the same. Celebrate noticing, not size.
- L4: Save vs Spend — pick one small want; watch Save if parent moves a bit (optional, parent-driven).
- L5: Grow for college — “this grows while you’re in school”; no return %.
- L6: Privacy — we don’t compare jars with friends or siblings as a contest.

**Screens**
1. **Who are you?** — Ella / Luca (pin or Face ID later; Phase 1 can be tap + parent trust).
2. **Home** — Hello [Name]. Three jar cards with big $. One line: “Your money has jobs.”
3. **Jar detail** — Name, balance, one sentence (“This is waiting for something you choose” / “This is growing for college and later”).
4. **Learn** — one lesson card at a time; checkmark when parent marks done.
5. **Parent corner** (hidden or passcode) — map accounts → jars; hide dollar amounts for Spend if desired; update lesson progress.

**Out of scope Phase 1:** allowance engine, interest calculator, transfers, charts, market %.

---

## Phase 2 — Ages ~11–13
**Curriculum:** goals (bike, camp), simple “money that works” (compounding as a story with a $10 → later example), Grow split: College vs Later (brokerage/Roth), one gentle line chart of Grow over months.
**Screens:** Home keeps three jars; Grow opens two shelves; Goals list; “How we grew” chart (parent-fed monthly totals OK).

## Phase 3 — Teens ~14–17
**Curriculum:** account types by real names, contribution history, taxes at a glance (“Roth already taxed”), path to age-21 package (Roth, 401k idea, savings, brokerage), optional allowance + simple interest on Save.
**Screens:** Account list under jars, history, Age-21 roadmap, optional Allowance (only when you greenlight).

## Phase 4 — ~18–21
Hand keys: same UI becomes their ownership checklist before funded accounts transfer/access.

## Reading shelf (Gene's picks, added 2026-09-30)
Two books Gene has read and likes. Neither is for the 8–10 dashboard; they come in when the ideas match the phase.

**I Will Teach You to Be Rich — Ramit Sethi** (the practical system)
- **Phase 2 (~11–13), parent-told:** "Spend a lot on what you love, cut back on what you don't." Ties straight to the Spend jar and piggy slider: choosing on purpose, no guilt.
- **Phase 3 (~14–17), kid reads chapters:** automate savings, the four buckets of a conscious spending plan (fixed costs, investing, saving goals, guilt-free spending), and Roth IRA / 401(k) / index funds by real names. Fits the age-21 roadmap screen.
- **Phase 4 (~18–21), full read before getting the keys:** they set up their own automation and spending plan before the funded accounts are handed over.

**How to Be Rich — J. Paul Getty** (the owner mindset)
- **Phase 3 (~16–17), selected chapters with a parent:** thinking like an owner, not just an earner; that wealth is about building and stewarding, not showing off.
- **Phase 4 (~18–21), full read plus one family talk:** what it means to be responsible for money that was set aside for you.

**Parent shelf now:** Ramit's conscious spending plan is the same idea as our $400 weekly dining-and-fun pot and the $15k/month target: spend freely inside the guilt-free bucket, automate the rest. Good to reread as the kids' lessons get deeper.

Order: Ramit before Getty. Practical habits first, then the mindset once they own real accounts.

## Data rules (parent)
- Never show household net worth, vacation fund, or siblings’ totals.
- 529 + kids Roth + kids brokerage + kids savings only.
- Ella/Luca Monarch 529 reconnect still parent ops — dashboard can use manual balances until sync is clean.

## Success for v1
Luca or Ella can open the iPad, pick their name, point to Spend/Save/Grow, and say what each is for — without reading “529” or “Roth.”

## Rev 3 product locks (2026-09-28)
- Spend / Save: show real $.
- Grow (college + later): progress only — no real balances (privacy + age-appropriate).
- Curriculum in situ: jar tips and contextual lines; Learn is light, not a lecture tab.
- Tone: official + kid-compelling (serious money product, not toy chrome).
